2f 5 · Shell margin — issue text to paste

The connector is read-only on this repo, so I couldn't file it directly. Everything below is ready to paste into a new issue — suggested title first, then the body. Label: design.

Title: Make .shell top/bottom margin responsive (clamp) so the footer isn't pushed off-screen on short viewports Body: ## Problem `.shell` uses a fixed `margin: 8vh auto` (extension/newtab.css). On short viewports this reserves 8% of the height at the top *and* bottom regardless of how tall the card actually is. Combined with a tall portrait photo (`.photo-portrait` is `height: min(65vh, 560px)`), the card plus the 32px-margin footer can push the footer below the fold on a typical laptop — so the credit/links row is only reachable by scrolling a page that is otherwise meant to be seen at a glance. On large displays the 8vh is doing real work; it's what gives the new tab its calm, unhurried feel. So the fix is not simply to reduce it. ## Suggested change ```css .shell { margin: clamp(24px, 8vh, 96px) auto; } ``` Keeps the current proportional spacing where it looks good, floors it at 24px so short viewports don't lose the top eighth, and caps it at 96px so very tall displays don't push the card uncomfortably far down. ## Testing notes Needs real measurement rather than eyeballing. Please check before and after at: - 1280x720 and 1366x768 with a **tall portrait** photo (the `.photo-portrait` tier, ratio > 1.35) and every optional card row present — chips, adoption fee, distance, updated, rescue link, and both card actions. That's the worst case for total card height. - The same viewports with a normal landscape photo, to confirm the layout doesn't now read as top-heavy. - A tall display (e.g. a 1440x2560 portrait monitor) to confirm the 96px cap still feels generous rather than cramped. - The first-run state (`#location-panel` visible, no card) and the no-results notice state. Both are much shorter than a card, and they're where a too-small top margin would show first. Ideal outcome: at 1280x720 with a tall portrait card, the footer is visible without scrolling — or at minimum measurably closer to the fold than today. The clamp() values are a starting point, not a conclusion. Adjust based on what the measurements actually show.

Open-ended — other things I'd change in newtab.css

Worth fixing

Inter is declared but never loaded. font-family: Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui — Fraunces ships as a woff2, Inter doesn't, so almost everyone gets system-ui. Either drop Inter from the stack so what you design against is what ships, or self-host a subset the way Fraunces already is.
No focus-visible styles anywhere. Every button sets border: 0, which in Chrome keeps the default ring but it lands off-brand against moss. One rule — :focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--moss); outline-offset: 2px } — covers buttons, links and the zip input, and it's the cheapest accessibility win in the file.
color-scheme: light is hard-pinned. A new tab is the most-seen surface in the browser and this one is a bright cream page at 2am. The palette already has the pieces for a dark variant (ink/paper/wall just swap roles); a prefers-color-scheme block redefining the eight custom properties would be ~15 lines and need no markup change.
Zero transitions. Every hover is an instant color snap — buttons, links, share menu items. transition: background-color .12s ease on the button/anchor rules would make the whole thing feel considered without touching layout.
The footer's five links compete with the card. Mono, underlined, and all the same weight, they're the busiest block on a page whose job is one cat. Dropping the underline until hover (or demoting Facebook/LinkedIn behind the credit line) would quiet it — and matters more if the tagline moves out of there per 25a/25b.
.shell { margin: 8vh auto } is fragile at short viewports. On a laptop with a tall portrait photo the card can push the footer well past the fold. margin: clamp(24px, 8vh, 96px) auto keeps the generous feel on big screens without wasting the top eighth on small ones.

Noted, but I'd leave alone

The rotated chips. rotate(-2deg) / rotate(1.5deg) is the one piece of real personality in the file and it earns the rubber-stamp metaphor. Keep.
The .card::before pin-hole dot. Subtle, cheap, and it's what sells the card as a physical thing on a wall. Keep.
The negative margin-top on .fee. -0.375rem is doing rhythm correction because the chips row above it has a fixed bottom margin whether or not chips exist. It's brittle but it's inside the card-layout question you said is being decided separately, so I've left it.

My pick

25a for the tagline — it makes the line part of the brand lockup instead of footer furniture, costs almost no vertical space, and unlike 25c it doesn't turn a new tab into a splash screen. 25b is the one to pick if you want people to actually feel it rather than just read it. For settings, 30a if you want the issue closed exactly as written; 30b if you're open to the panel gaining a line of copy.
